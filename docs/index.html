Systematic strategy research · negative result

Inside the Null

Eight trading strategies were generated by an LLM, fitted by differential evolution, and validated against surrogate data containing nothing. None of them survived. The interesting part is how close several came to looking like they had.

llm-strategy-search 40 invariants · 11,000+ optimiser fits August 2026

The premise, from Vincent Maciejewski's Why LLMs can't trade, is that a language model cannot tell a real market effect from a pattern that merely looks good in history — so don't ask it to. Use it as a structure generator inside an optimisation loop: the model proposes the shape of a strategy and declares parameter ranges, differential evolution picks the numbers, and statistics decide whether anything survived.

That division of labour was implemented literally. A strategy is a pure function of price data and a parameter vector; it declares ranges through param_space() and there is no code path by which it can hard-code a magnitude. Five strategies were then run through the same pipeline: a daily Bitcoin volatility-regime switch, a monthly S&P 500 trend overlay, three long-only factor rotations, and a daily bitcoin-versus-gold switch.

All eight failed. More usefully, seven of the eight produced cross-validated scores that the identical pipeline also produces from data with no exploitable structure at all — and the eighth only revealed itself as noise when the null was measured more precisely.

The central finding

What the machinery produces from nothing

A cross-validated score feels like an out-of-sample result: the strategy was scored on quarters it never saw. But the fitting happens inside the loop. What comes out isn't "how well does this rule generalise" — it is what score does this machinery produce when pointed at this data. That question has no natural scale until you ask what the same machinery produces when there is definitionally nothing to find.

Each strip below is one null distribution. Every grey tick is a complete cross-validation — 30 or 40 optimiser fits — run on a surrogate series built to contain no exploitable structure. The shaded band spans the 5th to 95th percentile. The teal diamond is the real result.

Read the block-bootstrap rows. On data with no exploitable structure whatsoever, the pipeline's typical output is a positive score between +0.04 and +0.78, and its best output reaches +0.75 to +1.84. Every real result falls inside that range. BTC's +0.780 is beaten by a noise surrogate scoring +1.241; the momentum strategy's +1.010 is beaten by one scoring +1.638.

The bitcoin-versus-gold strip is the clearest case in the set. Its null mean is +0.783 against a real score of +0.912 — the actual result barely clears the average surrogate, and the null's 95th percentile sits at +1.505, far above it. That null mean is the highest of the six, and the reason is visible in the underlying asset: in-sample bitcoin drifted upward at 90.4% a year with enormous volatility clustering. The block bootstrap preserves both, so a vol-gated, trend-filtered long-bitcoin rule harvests a great deal from data containing nothing.

The uncomfortable part

Hand me those surrogate numbers without saying where they came from and I would have called several of them promising strategies. They were generated from series that, by construction, contain nothing to predict.

Mechanism

Why noise scores positive

The null distributions are not centred on zero. That is not a bug — it is the most useful thing the exercise produced, and it has a specific cause.

Volatility scaling raises Sharpe without any forecasting ability

If a series is heteroskedastic — calm stretches and violent stretches, which every financial series is — then cutting exposure when realised volatility is high mechanically improves the Sharpe ratio. This is arithmetic, not prediction: you are shrinking the denominator during the periods that contribute most of the variance. It requires no information about future direction whatsoever.

Every structure written here contained a volatility or "calm" gate, because that is what the mechanism literature says to do. And the block bootstrap preserves volatility clustering — it resamples in blocks precisely so that it does. So the surrogates hand each strategy a real, harvestable statistical property that has nothing to do with the hypothesis under test. That is most of why the null means sit at +0.17 to +0.50 rather than at zero.

Plus optimiser freedom

Seven to nine parameters, refitted on every split, and blocked-quarter cross-validation leaves test quarters interleaved with training quarters that share the same regimes — so "held out" is not fully independent.

Where the article's method falls short

Sign-flipping deletes the drift

The published framework builds its null by flipping the sign of returns. That destroys the structure — but it also destroys the drift. The surrogate world is one where the asset didn't go up.

For a long-only strategy on a rising asset that is a rigged comparison in the strategy's favour. In the real world it collects the risk premium simply for being invested; in the surrogate world that premium is gone, so the bar is far too low. The strategy is credited for being long, which took no skill and no search.

The size of the distortion is visible in the null means for the same strategy, same pipeline, same data:

And that gap decided a case. The IUSE trend overlay cleared the sign-flip null at p = 0.038 and would have been reported as a validated strategy. It went on to lose to buy-and-hold in all thirteen holdout years — giving up six points of annual return for 1.4 points of drawdown, and in 2022, the one bear market it existed for, it de-risked after the fall and lost more than the index. The block-bootstrap null put it at p = 0.346, squarely inside the noise, and was right.

Across all five tests the block bootstrap was the stricter bar every time.

A near miss

The resolution floor

The momentum-versus-minimum-volatility rotation was the only strategy to pass every pre-registered gate. Its mechanism is the best-documented of the set: momentum does not fail randomly, it fails under an observable conjunction — market in drawdown and volatility high — because after a sustained decline the momentum book is implicitly short the beaten-down high-beta names and gets run over on the rebound. The rule held momentum only when the market was near its highs and calm. In 2022 it went to zero momentum weight and returned −9.5% against momentum's −18.3%. The mechanism visibly fired.

At 20 surrogates it cleared both null maxima at p = 0.048.

But 0.048 is 1/21 — the arithmetic floor of a 20-surrogate test. When the real score beats every draw, that is the smallest number the test can emit, no matter how strong or weak the effect is. It looks like significance and is actually the edge of the measuring instrument.

The null was extended to 40 surrogates with the target fixed in advance, so that finding a surrogate above the real score could not be waved away:

Both p-values got worse as evidence accumulated. That is the diagnostic. A genuine effect sharpens as surrogates pile up, because you are resolving a real gap more precisely. A resolution artifact degrades, because the gap was never there — there were simply too few draws to see the tail.

Everything else about the strategy was thin anyway: an active information ratio of +0.40 with a standard error of 0.42 on 68 monthly observations, giving t = 0.94 and a 95% interval running from −0.43 to +1.22. Deflated Sharpe, 0.084. And the sample cannot be extended — minimum-volatility ETFs did not exist before 2011.

Method

How a null bar is built

Three phrases, each doing specific work: take the real series, destroy the exploitable structure, keep everything else, and run the identical pipeline.

Destroy

Sign-flip chops returns into blocks, draws a fair coin per block, and negates the block on tails. Note what it does not touch: the magnitude at every bar is identical, so the volatility history is preserved exactly. Only direction is randomised — and with it, the drift.

Stationary block bootstrap rebuilds the series by stitching together randomly chosen runs of consecutive real returns, with geometric lengths and circular wrap-around. Every real return still appears, so the unconditional distribution — mean, variance, fat tails — survives. Dependence within a run survives; dependence across the stitch points does not.

For the rotations the construction is bespoke, because a rotation rule only ever earns w × (ret_A − ret_B). Each bar is split into a common component and a spread component, and only the spread's sign is flipped. The market move is preserved bar for bar; only which leg won is randomised. That zeroes out exactly the quantity being forecast, and nothing else.

Keep

Every preserved property is something the strategy could harvest without any forecasting skill, so removing it would hand the strategy an unearned win: bar count and calendar, so the cross-validation splits on identical quarter blocks; volatility clustering, because a vol gate profits from it arithmetically; the drift, because being long a rising asset is not a discovery; the cross-correlation of the two legs; and — for conditioned strategies — the exogenous series, left in its original time order, so that randomising the spread severs its link to rates or market state rather than carrying the relationship along intact.

Run identically

Same optimiser budget, population and iteration count, same split count and train fraction, same cost model, same scoring function, same parameter box. This is enforced structurally: the null calls the same cross-validation routine, which calls the same fitter. Giving the null a cheaper optimiser lowers the bar, and that is the single easiest way to fool yourself with this method.

Each surrogate yields one complete cross-validation collapsing to a single number. Forty surrogates give forty numbers, and p = (#{surrogates ≥ real} + 1) / (n + 1), where the +1s make it a valid conservative Monte-Carlo p-value.

The cost

Two families × 40 surrogates × 30 splits is 2,400 optimiser fits, against 30 for the actual result. The null bar is roughly 99% of the compute and the strategy backtest is a rounding error. That ratio is the honest price of knowing whether a number means anything — and it is probably why this step is the first thing dropped in practice.

Notes on each strategy

What actually happened

BTC-USD · daily volatility-regime switch

Trend-follow when realised vol is calm, buy washed-out RSI when it is violent, hard cash filter below the slow average. Long/flat only — UK retail cannot short crypto. Holdout Sharpe 0.96 against buy-and-hold's 0.86 looks like a win until you match the risk: at 44% average exposure the honest comparison is a constant 44% position, which scored 0.86 with a −27.0% drawdown against the strategy's −20.7%. The entire risk-adjusted edge came from being flat through one decline, in 2025–26.

SPY → IUSE.L · monthly trend overlay

Absolute momentum and a trend filter, scaled by a volatility percentile. Lost to buy-and-hold in all thirteen holdout years. Its own conditional breakdown gives the game away: in down months it lost 2.73% against the index's 3.63%, in up months it made 2.21% against 3.33%. It was a scaled-down version of the index, not a timed one.

RSP / SPY · equal weight versus cap weight

The three fixed allocations had effectively identical in-sample Sharpes — 0.68, 0.67, 0.68 — so equal weight's extra return was fully explained by its extra risk and no static tilt was worth having. Any edge had to come from timing. A perfect-foresight oracle would have earned +6.25%/yr of active return, so the opportunity was real; the rule captured −1.56%/yr of it. The spread's monthly autocorrelation is +0.113 at lag 1 and +0.003 at lag 3.

IVE / IVW · value versus growth, conditioned on the 10-year yield

Given a genuine exogenous driver — growth is long-duration equity, so its relative price tracks rates — the rate view was structured to take over only when rates were actually moving. Cross-validated score: −0.061. On the holdout the rule beat every fixed benchmark on Sharpe, but the entire result is 2022: +28.6 points against growth in the fastest tightening cycle in forty years. Strip out 2021–22 and the remaining ten years net −25.6 points. It was also a year early, paying −16.4 points in 2021 before the payoff arrived.

SPY ← ^VIX / ^VIX3M · equity exposure from the implied-vol curve

The only strategy here whose signal comes from option-implied expectations rather than realised price history, and the only one where the mechanism visibly worked and still did not pay. It cut exposure to its floor through the 2020 crash (−6.8% against SPY's −26.4%), the 2022 bear (−8.2% against −23.8%) and February 2018 (−2.0% against −6.3%) — the term structure inverted and the rule reacted, exactly as documented. But it was also underexposed through 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2024, and the forgone upside more than repaid the crashes it dodged. On the holdout it returned 5.75% a year at Sharpe 0.75 against 0.80 for both buy-and-hold and a passive constant 42% position. It did buy a genuinely smaller drawdown than the passive blend — −9.9% against −15.2% — but paid 0.6 points of annual return and 16 turns a year for it.

Its block-bootstrap null mean of +0.375 is the cleanest demonstration in this repo of why that null matters. This strategy is a volatility-timing rule, and the block bootstrap preserves volatility clustering — so the surrogates harvest most of what the real rule does, from data with nothing in it. In-sample the strategy looked spectacular (Sharpe 1.20 against 0.55, a −6.9% drawdown against −55.2%) because it dodged 2008. One event.

IWM / IWB · small caps versus large caps

The clearest failure in the set, and the least surprising. The size premium is already largely gone — the spread is +1.15%/yr over 26 years at 1.25x the volatility — so anything found had to come from timing the cycle. The cross-validated score was −0.535, negative on 0 of 25 splits, and 29 of 30 surrogates beat the real data in both null families. On the holdout, passively holding the same 19% average small-cap weight matched the strategy exactly — Sharpe 0.84 against 0.83, with no trading — and simply owning large caps beat both at 0.91. Small caps beat large by +3.65%/yr in-sample and lost 2.71%/yr out, so the optimiser learned precisely the wrong prior.

With no split generalising, the deployment routine hit its documented fallback and took the median across all fits, there being no generalising subset to select from. This was also the third outing for the momentum-plus-reversion-plus-volatility-gate structure, after equal-weight/cap-weight and value/growth. Three failures on the same shape is itself a result: the structures available from a causal, price-only indicator library are not unlimited, and running that one on a fourth spread would not be an independent test of anything.

MTUM / USMV · momentum versus minimum volatility

The best-motivated structure and the only one to reach the final gate. Covered above. Worth noting that this was the one pair whose spread did not flip sign between windows — momentum beat min-vol by +5.17%/yr in-sample and +5.60%/yr out — which makes it a more stable relationship, but also means a naive permanent tilt captured most of the return while the strategy gave up 2%/yr of CAGR versus simply holding momentum.

BTC-USD / GLD · bitcoin versus gold, daily

Run daily rather than monthly because the common history starts at bitcoin's inception and a monthly test would have had ~143 observations. Bitcoin runs 4.1x gold's volatility at a correlation of 0.090, so a 50/50 weight portfolio is roughly 94% bitcoin risk — which is why this strategy's kill criterion named the risk-matched constant-weight blend in advance. It needed to: holding the same 32% average weight passively returned the same 19.5% a year at a third less volatility, a smaller drawdown and no trading, and simply holding gold beat everything with a Sharpe of 1.07. The trading destroyed value. In-sample bitcoin returned 90.4%/yr against gold's 6.1%, so the optimiser learned "be in bitcoin"; out of sample the two were level at 20.1% and 20.4%.

Average exposure is a misleading summary

This strategy lost 24.5% in 2022 while averaging a 3% bitcoin weight, in a year gold was flat. That is not a bug. It held a full-size bitcoin position on exactly eight days, and bitcoin averaged −3.5% on those days against −0.33% across the year — including 82% weight on 8 November, the day FTX collapsed, and 100% weight on 13 September for −9.3%.

A strategy that is usually flat and occasionally all-in is not described by its mean weight. Report the position-weighted return of the leg beside it: here that was −25.1% against the strategy's −22.1% gross.

A pattern across the rotations

Both style spreads reversed sign between the fitting and holdout windows — equal weight beat cap weight by +1.94%/yr in-sample then lost 2.83%/yr out; value beat growth by +2.00%/yr then lost 5.25%/yr. Anything fitted on the first window learned the wrong prior for the second. In both cases the optimiser also discarded the mean-reversion leg entirely and pinned two parameters to the edges of their declared boxes — a reliable sign that the structure cannot express whatever the search is chasing.

The harness

What the invariants caught

Thirty-six invariants run before any result is trusted. The one that matters most rewrites the entire future of a price series and asserts the signal history up to the cut is byte-identical. Four real bugs surfaced this way, each of which would have corrupted results silently:

  • Surrogate price paths were not anchored to the starting price, so block-bootstrap surrogates began at a different level than the series they were calibrating.
  • The deflated-Sharpe implementation was missing its variance term and returned approximately zero for every input — a gate that always fails is as useless as one that always passes.
  • The indicator cache was keyed on id(), which never hits (pandas hands back a new object each call) and can collide after garbage collection.
  • The replacement cache fingerprinted arrays by length and endpoints — so pctile(x) and pctile(|x|) collided whenever the last value was positive, and one was silently served the other's result. The look-ahead test caught it because rewriting the future flips that sign and thereby changed past values.

Two of my own analysis errors were caught the same way: a "perfect foresight" oracle that was silently lagged a bar by the execution convention, making it a naive momentum rule; and a correlation that returned NaN because US month-end dates were being joined against LSE dates one day later.

A performance note, since it determines what is affordable: moving the indicator layer from pandas to numpy took a single optimiser fit from 205 seconds to 11. The null bars are only feasible because of it.

Carrying forward

Four rules

  • A surrogate null must preserve everything the strategy could harvest for free, and destroy only what it claims to forecast. Drift and volatility clustering are free. If the null removes them you are testing whether being invested beats not being invested, which you already know.
  • Size the null before you look at it. Twenty surrogates can never report better than p = 0.048; forty reaches 0.024. Choose the count from the p-value you need to resolve, and fix it in advance.
  • A cross-validated score is a property of the procedure, not the rule. It needs calibrating against what the same procedure yields on nothing before it means anything at all.
  • A parameter pinned to the edge of its declared range is a diagnostic. It usually means the structure cannot express what the optimiser is chasing.
  • Benchmark against the risk-matched constant weight, not just the legs. Any strategy that lowers its average exposure cuts volatility and flatters its Sharpe for reasons unrelated to skill. Name that benchmark in the kill criterion before running, not after seeing the result.

The framework itself is the durable output. Swapping in a new hypothesis is one file implementing param_space() and structure(); everything else — costs, execution lag, the two null families, the holdout discipline — comes along unchanged. It has now killed eight strategies, which is what it is for.
